# Components and Actions

Read [Buttons and CTAs](#buttons-and-ctas-cta), [Cards](#cards-card), [Accordions and tabs](#accordions-and-tabs-disc), or [Dialogs and drawers](#dialogs-and-drawers-ovr). These 40 suggestions change action/context relationships and disclosure, not the design system's primitive styling.

Inspect installed component APIs and existing variants before composing. An independently useful primitive belongs in its existing shared owner even with one current app. A feature-specific wrapper can supply app behavior without creating a second primitive.

## Buttons and CTAs (CTA)

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CTA-01 | Verb-and-object action: a specific label sits directly beside the thing it changes. | A clear local operation such as adding a member or exporting a report. | Several indistinguishable "Continue" buttons obscure consequences. | Reuse the existing variant and size; allow label growth and keep sufficient surrounding space for safe activation. |
| CTA-02 | Primary action plus low-commitment alternative: one action is emphasized while a meaningful secondary path remains available. | Visitors differ in readiness to buy, book, or try. | Two equally dominant actions compete for the same task. | Preserve visual priority through existing variants; stack by importance on mobile without hiding the alternative. |
| CTA-03 | Action with consequence context: explanatory text names price, access, or next step before commitment. | Signup, purchase, or an external handoff has material conditions. | Microcopy becomes a substitute for a clear label. | Keep conditions readable outside the button; reflect the actual product terms and error state. |
| CTA-04 | Contextual sticky action: the current choice and next step remain accessible during a long decision. | Configurators, booking, or a lengthy valid form. | The sticky region obscures content or duplicates multiple persistent bars. | Account for safe areas and zoom; show the relevant summary and avoid covering fields, errors, or focus. |
| CTA-05 | Split action: a frequent default action has an adjacent menu for true alternatives. | A stable main action has related variants such as export formats. | There is no safe or predictable default. | Use separate labeled activation targets and the installed menu API; keep the default action obvious. |
| CTA-06 | Stateful toggle action: an immediately reversible mode exposes its current on/off state in the action itself. | Pinning, following, or another supported binary preference. | The operation is one-way or has consequences beyond a simple toggle. | Use the installed toggle semantics and explicit state; do not communicate selection through color alone or imply success before confirmation. |
| CTA-07 | Reversible action with inline recovery: a low-risk change confirms its result and offers undo when actually supported. | Archive, move, or non-destructive organization tasks. | The action cannot truly be reversed. | Use the real recovery capability; preserve focus and expose failure without falsely reporting success. |
| CTA-08 | Form-entry CTA: a compact labeled field begins a relevant task immediately. | Search, subscription, postcode availability, or another short initial step. | Sensitive or complex data needs a dedicated flow. | Keep the submit action, validation, and success state connected; do not substitute placeholder text for a label. |
| CTA-09 | Destination preview action: a link states the resource or route it opens, with useful metadata nearby. | Downloads, external resources, menus, or documents. | Every ordinary link gets an ornamental badge and arrow. | Use link semantics for navigation; only show format, size, or destination facts that are known. |
| CTA-10 | Risk-separated confirmation: destructive commitment is distinguished from the everyday workflow. | Irreversible deletion, publishing, or other consequential operations. | A simple reversible action is needlessly interrupted. | Name the affected object and consequence; use the system's destructive variant and established confirmation behavior. |

## Cards (CARD)

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CARD-01 | Object summary: identity, status, essential attributes, and one clear detail destination. | A collection of real objects supports browsing. | Users mainly need row-by-row numerical comparison. | Use consistent field priority; keep secondary actions separate from the primary link without nested interactive elements. |
| CARD-02 | Media record: a meaningful image and caption orient users before the metadata. | Places, products, or projects are visually distinguishable. | Stock photos make unrelated records look identical. | Preserve useful crops, descriptions, and loading space; place critical information in text rather than over imagery. |
| CARD-03 | Actionable exception: a problem, its impact, and the next valid action share one compact surface. | An item requires intervention rather than passive monitoring. | Every ordinary item is styled as urgent. | Use semantic status and clear text; show resolved/unavailable states and avoid color-only meaning. |
| CARD-04 | Progress summary: current stage, remaining work, and a resume action form the hierarchy. | A real process continues across sessions. | A decorative completion percentage has no defined calculation. | Identify the next step and any blocking condition; keep the resume path usable when progress data is missing. |
| CARD-05 | Comparison-ready option: repeated attributes line up across a small set of choices. | Plans, service tiers, or configurations have meaningful tradeoffs. | Unequal text and decoration prevent scanning the criteria. | Keep criteria and units consistent; on small screens preserve comparison context rather than hiding key restrictions. |
| CARD-06 | Detail-on-demand record: a concise summary expands for secondary attributes in place. | Most items need scanning but a few need inspection. | Expansion would contain a whole workflow. | Use a separate disclosure trigger, maintain expanded state where appropriate, and keep focus stable after updates. |
| CARD-07 | Timeline event card: an entry is anchored to time, actor, and change. | Activity or case histories require sequence and accountability. | Every item uses a large decorative portrait instead of explaining the event. | Include factual timestamps and grouping; maintain a chronological reading order on all screen sizes. |
| CARD-08 | Content excerpt: title, subject, and a useful preview establish relevance before opening. | Articles, documents, or learning materials. | Truncation removes the only meaningful distinction between items. | Let titles wrap and differentiate link names; keep actual excerpts separate from fabricated summaries. |
| CARD-09 | Relationship card: one record is presented through its meaningful connection to the current item. | Assigned people, linked projects, or related services. | A relation is inferred without product support. | Name the relationship, not just the object; provide an accessible direct route and handle missing related data. |
| CARD-10 | Collection entry with compact metadata: one representative item and the collection's scope act as a browse doorway. | Albums, project folders, or resource groups. | A collage hides the group name or misrepresents its contents. | Keep counts factual, identify empty collections, and preserve a clear open-collection action on mobile. |

## Accordions and tabs (DISC)

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DISC-01 | Question accordion: explicit questions reveal concise supporting answers. | Secondary explanations or FAQs interrupt a primary reading path. | Answers contain information everyone must see before acting. | Use proper disclosure semantics; allow text growth and keep meaningful content available without hover. |
| DISC-02 | Section disclosure with summary: the closed heading includes the current configuration or essential result. | Dense settings or record sections need selective inspection. | The summary becomes a tiny unreadable paragraph. | Keep required errors discoverable and open affected sections; retain a stable trigger and expanded state. |
| DISC-03 | Local record tabs: stable views separate different aspects of one object. | A record has distinct overview, activity, or configuration tasks. | The tabs form a sequential wizard or unrelated site navigation. | Match route links versus tab semantics to actual behavior; support long labels and preserve relevant state. |
| DISC-04 | Inline reference notes: a named citation or note marker opens supporting context without replacing the main reading position. | Research, policies, or explanatory content has occasional supporting detail. | The note contains an essential instruction everyone must read. | Keep a reachable full-note destination, stable focus return, and a readable expanded or endnote alternative on small screens. |
| DISC-05 | Inline technical details: a brief result exposes logs, formulas, or implementation context on request. | Experts sometimes need evidence behind a simple answer. | Errors are hidden under a vague "More" label. | Name the detail explicitly; keep logs selectable and horizontally scrollable without breaking the whole page. |
| DISC-06 | Expandable table detail: a row opens supporting attributes while primary columns remain aligned. | Occasional investigation complements regular row comparison. | Nested detail includes another full data application. | Use a dedicated expansion control; preserve column context and route to a detail page for substantial work. |
| DISC-07 | Optional field group: uncommon settings remain behind a clearly named disclosure. | Most users succeed with defaults but a minority need control. | Required fields or consent are being obscured. | Expose validation errors within hidden groups and keep chosen advanced values visible in a concise summary. |
| DISC-08 | Outline with collapsible subsections: hierarchical content supports scanning at different depths. | Long reference material has real nested topics. | Repeated nesting becomes a maze of toggles. | Preserve heading structure and deep-link access; keep important first-level information visible. |
| DISC-09 | Mobile navigation disclosure: labeled groups open within the navigation surface. | A desktop mega menu needs a readable mobile equivalent. | Every single link is unnecessarily hidden one level deeper. | Preserve top-level destination access and selected-path context; avoid duplicating a toggle and link ambiguously. |
| DISC-10 | View switch with stable scope: list, calendar, or board share the same collection and filters. | Different representations support distinct valid tasks. | Switching views silently changes the dataset. | Label the active view and preserve filters/selection where supported; do not style a mode switch as unrelated content tabs. |

## Dialogs and drawers (OVR)

Keep titles/descriptions and close controls in the installed compound primitives so accessible relationships remain intact. A modal needs managed focus and a reliable exit; non-modal inspectors need a clear focus path rather than an accidental focus trap. Choose modality deliberately. Never place a floating close icon over a long title or require pointer-only dismissal.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OVR-01 | Focused confirmation dialog: object, consequence, and explicit decision fit one short interruption. | A consequential action needs an informed final choice. | Routine interactions become repeated confirmation friction. | Use the established modal with clear primary/cancel labels; handle long object names and return focus predictably. |
| OVR-02 | Detail drawer: inspect one record while keeping the collection context visible. | Fast triage or comparison followed by a return to the list. | The task needs deep navigation or many persistent sections. | Preserve the selected record and scroll position; use a clear full-page detail option on small screens if needed. |
| OVR-03 | Quick-create dialog: minimal necessary fields create an object without leaving the current workflow. | A related object must be added during another task. | Creation requires extensive configuration and explanation. | Keep errors in context, preserve entered values, and provide the actual created-object result without nested modal stacks. |
| OVR-04 | Filter sheet: grouped criteria and selected scope occupy one temporary surface. | Mobile or space-constrained lists have several filters. | A simple one-field control becomes a full overlay. | Match apply/reset to the product's filter behavior; retain values and make active filters visible after closing. |
| OVR-05 | Resource chooser: search and selection attach existing objects to the current task without leaving its context. | Assigning members, choosing files, or linking existing records. | Users must inspect a complex object in depth before choosing it. | Keep query, selection, and confirm/cancel roles clear; preserve the parent form and distinguish unavailable choices from an empty collection. |
| OVR-06 | Media lightbox: one selected image gains inspection space with related context. | Genuine image detail matters to evaluation. | The enlarged asset has no extra resolution or meaning. | Keep captions, close, zoom alternatives, and navigation accessible; do not trap mobile scrolling behind a decorative frame. |
| OVR-07 | Anchored action popover: a small choice appears adjacent to its trigger and affected content. | A compact menu or local option needs immediate context. | The surface contains a long form or nested workflow. | Use the real menu/popover component; manage viewport edges, Escape, focus return, and touch activation. |
| OVR-08 | Comparison tray: selected objects persist at the edge before a deliberate comparison view opens. | Users collect a few alternatives from a larger catalog. | The tray occupies more attention than the browsing task. | State selected count and clear/remove actions; avoid covering content and preserve a direct mobile comparison path. |
| OVR-09 | Contextual help panel: task-relevant explanation remains available beside the work. | Complex fields or procedures need optional guidance. | The help panel is unsolicited promotional content. | Prefer non-modal behavior when work can continue; allow closing and link to full guidance when space is insufficient. |
| OVR-10 | Activity drawer: changes or notifications are inspected without navigating away from the current record. | Users need event context during ongoing work. | Critical alerts are hidden until the drawer is opened. | Separate read state from resolved state; keep urgent requirements visible elsewhere and prevent noisy focus-stealing updates. |
