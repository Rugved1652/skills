# Content and Behavior

Read [Typography and content hierarchy](#typography-and-content-hierarchy-type), [Imagery and media](#imagery-and-media-media), [Onboarding and empty states](#onboarding-and-empty-states-state), or [Motion and responsive transitions](#motion-and-responsive-transitions-mot). The 40 suggestions adapt content and state within the existing system. They do not introduce another font scale, animation library, or set of visual tokens.

## Typography and content hierarchy (TYPE)

Use installed Typography APIs and semantic headings. Operational headings keep the application scale; a marketing display treatment requires deliberate support from the system. Important explanatory text is not made tiny or low contrast to make the title appear sophisticated. Retain existing fonts, including Inter or monospace when appropriate.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TYPE-01 | Task hierarchy: page purpose, immediate instruction, and action establish a short reading sequence. | Work pages where users need rapid orientation. | A decorative slogan delays the actual task. | Use existing heading/body roles and meaningful language; allow wrapping instead of shrinking supporting text. |
| TYPE-02 | Editorial measure: a readable main text column is supported by quieter captions or marginal notes. | Articles, explanations, or case studies. | Every line is stretched across a large screen. | Follow the existing type and spacing scale; move marginal content next to its related paragraph on narrow screens. |
| TYPE-03 | Action-and-evidence pairing: each claim is followed closely by the artifact or fact supporting it. | Product or service descriptions need credibility. | Unverified claims are visually amplified by oversized type. | Preserve source/context text and semantic reading order; use emphasis through grouping rather than new fonts. |
| TYPE-04 | Numeric reading hierarchy: a measure stays visually connected to its unit, period, and label. | Financial, operational, or analytical views. | A giant number hides its denominator or meaning. | Use available number styles and alignment conventions; avoid ambiguous truncation and expose complete values where needed. |
| TYPE-05 | Definition list: stable labels and variable values form readable record details. | Metadata, specifications, or account information. | A card is created for every key/value pair. | Use aligned groups or semantic definitions; stack label/value pairs on narrow screens and handle long values. |
| TYPE-06 | Scan-first instruction: a concise outcome precedes grouped steps or requirements. | Procedures, setup, or guidance alongside a task. | Several long paragraphs hide the first actionable step. | Keep real sequence and heading levels; ensure numbered steps are content order rather than decorative badges. |
| TYPE-07 | Chosen emphasis within a headline: one meaningful phrase gains emphasis through existing weight or treatment. | A proposition has a clear semantic pivot. | Arbitrary gradient words or manual line breaks manufacture personality. | Keep the sentence comprehensible without styling; avoid fixed breaks that fail under translation or zoom. |
| TYPE-08 | Status-and-explanation pair: a concise state label is accompanied by its consequence or next step. | Error, pending, blocked, or completed task states. | A colored badge leaves users guessing what to do. | Use the semantic state vocabulary and normal readable copy; keep details discoverable without color recognition. |
| TYPE-09 | Layered technical content: a plain-language result precedes exact technical identifiers or supporting output. | Developer tools and advanced configuration. | Monospace is used for every heading to imply sophistication. | Reserve code treatment for actual code/identifiers under existing tokens; allow copying and controlled horizontal overflow. |
| TYPE-10 | Captioned section boundary: a descriptive heading and short orienting sentence introduce the next content responsibility. | A long page moves between distinct questions or tasks. | Repetitive tiny eyebrow labels add no orientation. | Maintain a coherent heading hierarchy and spacing rhythm; keep context readable on mobile rather than ornamental. |

## Imagery and media (MEDIA)

Use authentic, relevant assets the project owns or the user has authorized. Do not copy gallery images or imply fabricated people, premises, products, or customer proof are real. Choose a content-first composition when the needed asset does not exist. Media must earn its space by explaining, orienting, or demonstrating.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MEDIA-01 | Context photograph: a real environment explains where a service or product is used. | Hospitality, spaces, events, or physical work. | A generic image could represent any business. | Preserve a meaningful subject crop and text contrast; provide appropriate image description and loading fallback. |
| MEDIA-02 | Product detail image: a useful close view communicates material, construction, or function. | Physical products or craft with visible differentiators. | Detail photography conceals the actual product scale or form. | Pair with orientation context and honest captions; ensure the crop remains informative on small screens. |
| MEDIA-03 | Authentic interface capture: a real workflow screenshot supports a specific capability claim. | Software is understood through its actual interaction. | A tilted mockup hides content or promises a nonexistent feature. | Highlight the relevant task without unreadably shrinking the whole app; supply textual explanation and protect private data. |
| MEDIA-04 | Annotated process diagram: a few labeled relationships explain a real flow or system. | A sequence, dependency, or architecture needs clarification. | Boxes and arrows merely decorate ordinary prose. | Keep a complete linear explanation; preserve labels and use a stacked version when the diagram becomes cramped. |
| MEDIA-05 | Object-on-neutral presentation: one product/artifact is isolated for clear inspection. | The object's form or details are the main content. | An unrelated floating 3D object stands in for meaning. | Use existing surfaces and effects; preserve scale cues, descriptions, and a stable layout while assets load. |
| MEDIA-06 | Paired evidence images: two legitimately comparable states appear with explicit labels and conditions. | A real transformation or comparison is available. | Crops or lighting manufacture an unsupported improvement claim. | Provide both states without a drag requirement; a slider, if used, needs keyboard controls and visible labels. |
| MEDIA-07 | Documentary image sequence: selected images reveal different parts of one real experience. | Venues, projects, food preparation, or events. | A collage repeats the same image solely to fill space. | Preserve captions, sequence, and useful aspect ratios; simplify to a readable ordered set on mobile. |
| MEDIA-08 | Functional illustration: a diagrammatic visual explains an otherwise hard-to-see concept. | An abstract relationship cannot be conveyed by a photograph. | Mascots and blobs add no information. | Match existing illustration language and provide text equivalence; avoid creating a new visual identity for one screen. |
| MEDIA-09 | Purposeful video preview: a relevant moment invites user-controlled playback with context. | Demonstrating movement or an environment adds real understanding. | Autoplay spectacle delays or obscures the primary task. | Use an honest poster and appropriate captions/controls; keep the page useful without video or automatic motion. |
| MEDIA-10 | Data as visual evidence: a real chart, map, or measured artifact anchors an explanatory section. | Product value can be shown through actual evidence. | Decorative graphs invent results or omit essential context. | State source, units, and period where relevant; retain accessible values and a meaningful non-visual explanation. |

## Onboarding and empty states (STATE)

Distinguish first use, a genuinely empty collection, no search matches, missing access, loading, failure, and stale data. They are not interchangeable illustrations with a "Get started" button. Do not invent product capabilities to fill a state.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STATE-01 | First-object invitation: describe the collection and offer its valid first creation path. | A new workspace legitimately has no records. | Users lack permission to create the object. | Tailor the action to real access; keep explanatory text useful without an oversized decorative illustration. |
| STATE-02 | Constraint-aware no-results state: show the active scope and a precise way to broaden it. | Search/filter combinations yield no matches. | The underlying request actually failed. | Preserve query and filters; label each reset's effect and do not imply all data was deleted. |
| STATE-03 | Task-relevant setup checklist: real prerequisites lead toward the first meaningful outcome. | The product needs a few known configuration steps. | An arbitrary checklist becomes the new dashboard. | Indicate optional steps, preserve progress, and let users return to work when setup is not mandatory. |
| STATE-04 | Progressive in-context introduction: explain an unfamiliar control when the user reaches its task. | One capability needs explanation beyond its label. | Tooltips interrupt every routine interaction. | Make hints dismissible and keyboard-usable; do not hide necessary instructions only inside ephemeral overlays. |
| STATE-05 | Demonstration sandbox: clearly labeled example data lets users explore before connecting their own source. | The product actually supports an isolated sample environment. | Sample records could be mistaken for real customer activity. | Separate sample and real contexts visibly; explain reset/exit behavior and never fabricate a successful integration. |
| STATE-06 | Limited-access state: explain the available route when the current user cannot act or inspect content. | Existing authorization legitimately restricts the task. | A redesign invents a new role or permission policy. | Use known access rules, avoid exposing sensitive details, and offer a real support/request path only if supported. |
| STATE-07 | Local recoverable failure: an affected module offers context and a safe next attempt without replacing the whole screen. | One request fails while the rest of the workspace remains usable. | Retrying may repeat a consequential mutation. | Preserve user input and distinguish read retry from mutation reconciliation; keep errors and next steps accessible. |
| STATE-08 | Structure-preserving loading: placeholders reserve the space of the expected content. | A short wait should not cause major reflow. | Skeletons misrepresent unknown content or continue indefinitely. | Keep motion optional, avoid announcing each placeholder, and transition to real empty/error states when known. |
| STATE-09 | Completion with continuation: the real result is confirmed with its reference and a useful next step. | Submission or creation has actually completed. | Confetti or optimism reports success before confirmation. | State what happened and where to inspect it; keep recovery or correction routes clear when supported. |
| STATE-10 | Stale-but-usable content: known prior data remains visible with freshness and update context. | Partial connectivity or refresh failure leaves useful cached information. | Stale values would be unsafe to treat as current. | Use actual freshness semantics; make limitations clear and avoid automatic resorting that disorients ongoing work. |

## Motion and responsive transitions (MOT)

Use the applicable animation skill and the design system's timing/effects. Check both CSS and programmatic animations: disabling CSS keyframes alone does not stop scripted motion or autoplay. No pattern requires animation when an immediate state change is clearer. Avoid scroll hijacking, cursor gimmicks, focus theft, and delayed access to content.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MOT-01 | Sidebar compression: the same navigation changes between labeled width and compact rail while maintaining orientation. | Users intentionally reclaim workspace. | Every collapse forces expensive or distracting content movement. | Preserve active item/focus; use system timing and an immediate reduced-motion state without hover-only labels. |
| MOT-02 | Drawer continuity: a detail surface enters from the relevant edge while the selected object remains identifiable. | Inspection should retain the parent collection context. | A full route transition is being disguised as a temporary overlay. | Manage modality/focus through the primitive; mobile may use a sheet or page, and motion must not delay access. |
| MOT-03 | Disclosure expansion: a section changes height while its heading stays an orientation anchor. | Users deliberately reveal secondary information. | Animating large content repeatedly causes reading to jump. | Expose state immediately, preserve keyboard focus, and skip interpolation when reduced motion or performance warrants it. |
| MOT-04 | Context-preserving view change: shared scope and selected item connect a list, board, or calendar transition. | Switching representations of the same data. | A cinematic transition hides different datasets or reset filters. | Keep state semantics first; use an immediate view switch as the complete reduced-motion alternative. |
| MOT-05 | Local save feedback: only the affected field or action changes to acknowledge progress and result. | Saving or updating one bounded part of a screen. | A full-screen animation interrupts unrelated work. | Pair motion with accessible status text, preserve focus, and handle real pending/failure states without false completion. |
| MOT-06 | Stable live update: new data is signaled without silently relocating the item being inspected. | Monitoring, event feeds, or collaborative lists. | Every refresh triggers a global staggered entrance. | Offer an explicit update/reorder affordance when needed; preserve selection and avoid excessive announcements. |
| MOT-07 | Breakpoint reorganization: related regions shift from side-by-side to a deliberate linear task order. | A sidebar, inspector, or media/text layout reaches its content limit. | Desktop coordinates are simply scaled down into unreadability. | Preserve semantic DOM order, state, and focus; do not require animated rearrangement to explain the result. |
| MOT-08 | Restrained section reveal: a nonessential visual transition supports a deliberate editorial sequence. | An expressive marketing brief benefits from paced emphasis. | Text stays hidden until a scroll trigger or users need immediate scanning. | Content remains available without scripts/motion; avoid repeated reveals and never hold essential actions behind a timeline. |
| MOT-09 | Reorder feedback: movement shows a confirmed item's old/new position within a collection. | The product supports sorting or manual reordering. | Dragging is the only way to change order. | Offer keyboard/button alternatives and textual confirmation; keep reduced-motion users oriented without travel animation. |
| MOT-10 | Media-to-detail transition: a selected artifact retains identity while opening a fuller view. | Galleries or portfolios benefit from continuity between preview and inspection. | A dramatic zoom adds latency or harms motion-sensitive users. | Keep ordinary navigation/focus behavior, permit a direct transition, and ensure the destination is usable if animation fails. |
