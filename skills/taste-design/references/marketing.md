# Marketing Composition

Read the relevant category: [Heroes](#heroes-hero), [Section sequences](#section-sequences-sec), [Carousels](#carousels-car), or [Modular grids](#modular-grids-grid). The 40 entries are independent suggestions, not a prescribed landing-page order. Use the current design system and real content; see [research](research.md) for reference interpretation.

## Heroes (HERO)

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HERO-01 | Editorial opening: a clear proposition leads into one specific supporting fact and action, without a compulsory illustration. | Services whose language and credibility carry the decision. | Copy is vague and decorative scale would conceal the problem. | Preserve the font family; derive line breaks from content width and let narrow-screen text wrap naturally. |
| HERO-02 | Demonstration-first: a useful product interaction or authentic walkthrough leads, with a concise explanation alongside or below. | A product is easier to understand by seeing its central task. | A fabricated screenshot promises unavailable behavior. | Provide a meaningful static fallback, label demos honestly, and keep the main action usable without playback. |
| HERO-03 | Integrated media field: a headline occupies a deliberate open region within an image-led composition. | Strong, relevant imagery supports the proposition. | Text readability depends on one fragile image crop. | Preserve system contrast and shape rules; separate text from imagery on small screens when overlap becomes cramped. |
| HERO-04 | Primary field plus secondary rail: one dominant story shares the opening with smaller, distinct practical destinations. | A main narrative coexists with booking, locations, or timely information. | Every item claims equal priority or the rail is filler. | Give regions clear weights; linearize by importance on mobile and preserve labels independently of imagery. |
| HERO-05 | Search or booking opener: the useful task begins in the hero instead of after a promotional pitch. | Restaurants, travel, directories, appointments, or resource discovery. | The form asks for extensive commitment before users understand the offer. | Keep essential fields only; make labels, validation, date/location context, and keyboard completion visible. |
| HERO-06 | Evidence-led opening: a verified result or concrete artifact anchors the proposition. | A service can explain value through a credible outcome. | A large invented metric is being used as decoration. | Name the context/source and period where relevant; retain a text explanation when an image or chart cannot load. |
| HERO-07 | Audience pathways: a short shared promise leads directly to different valid journeys. | Distinct audiences have clearly different needs. | Users must guess which invented persona fits them. | Use task language and equal access to alternatives; mobile pathways remain explicit rather than hidden in a carousel. |
| HERO-08 | Annotated object: one real product, space, or artifact is explained through restrained adjacent callouts. | Physical products, architecture, or a specific technical capability. | Callouts merely rename obvious visual parts. | Keep annotations outside critical image areas; convert them to an ordered list on narrow screens. |
| HERO-09 | Live collection opening: a featured item introduces a browsable catalog immediately. | Media, projects, menus, or portfolios where examples are the proposition. | The collection is too thin to sustain browsing. | Identify the featured item and collection boundary; preserve straightforward links and a stable reading order. |
| HERO-10 | Event or availability opening: date, place, status, and next action establish an immediate practical context. | Events, launches, classes, or capacity-based services. | Artificial urgency or a fake countdown manufactures relevance. | Use real dates/time zones and meaningful sold-out/ended states; keep details readable without animated counters. |

## Section sequences (SEC)

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SEC-01 | Question-and-evidence sequence: each section resolves a visitor question with specific content. | Complex services or considered purchases. | Sections are generic slogans without evidence. | Let the actual questions set section length and layout; preserve headings for direct scanning. |
| SEC-02 | Process journey: stages explain what happens before, during, and after an action. | Services with uncertainty about procedure or handoff. | The stages are invented to make a familiar task look elaborate. | Use a readable sequence before adding motion; stack steps without making horizontal swiping mandatory. |
| SEC-03 | Anchored index with deep content: a stable topic list accompanies one longer explanatory region. | Several services or capabilities need detailed explanation. | Content is short enough for a simple list. | Maintain active context and real anchors; on mobile place a concise index before content rather than shrinking both columns. |
| SEC-04 | Evidence ledger: claims align with artifacts, examples, or measured outcomes in adjacent rows. | Credibility matters more than decorative feature icons. | Evidence is unavailable or repeated across every claim. | Separate claim from source/context; collapse rows into clearly labeled pairs on narrow screens. |
| SEC-05 | Comparison spread: alternatives share aligned criteria across a restrained matrix or paired columns. | Users need to understand genuine tradeoffs. | The comparison misrepresents competitors or hides limitations. | Keep criteria consistent; use a readable table or stacked criterion groups rather than compressed marketing cards. |
| SEC-06 | Immersive example then explanation: a case, environment, or artifact precedes its interpretation. | Experience-led work such as hospitality, architecture, or craft. | Media substitutes for answering a concrete service question. | Provide useful captions and a deliberate crop; preserve a logical text-first fallback when media is unavailable. |
| SEC-07 | Capability atlas: named capabilities surround or link to a meaningful organizing model. | Services form a real workflow, lifecycle, or ecosystem. | A diagram would invent relationships users do not need. | Keep relationships readable in text and links; replace a sprawling diagram with a linear grouped view on mobile. |
| SEC-08 | Objection-to-action sequence: answer a specific concern immediately before the relevant next step. | Pricing, onboarding, trust, or implementation concerns block action. | Every answer ends in a competing aggressive CTA. | Use accurate terms and supporting detail; retain the same action vocabulary across the page. |
| SEC-09 | Chaptered long-form story: varied text, images, and quotations share a consistent column rhythm. | Editorial, research, or case-study storytelling. | Every chapter gets a different theme and interaction gimmick. | Keep one grid and coherent type hierarchy; use quotations only when supplied and attributable. |
| SEC-10 | Practical close: location, access, hours, requirements, and contact answer the final operational questions. | Services and venues with real visit or booking constraints. | A generic inspirational banner would delay the needed information. | Make details selectable and links actionable; prioritize directions/contact for the small-screen context. |

## Carousels (CAR)

Use a carousel when sequential exploration serves the content, not to hide essential information. Prefer manual movement. A horizontal multi-card list and a one-slide-at-a-time carousel need different visibility/focus treatment: offscreen actions must not become unreachable or invisible focus traps. Follow the installed component's semantics rather than adding conflicting roles.

For automatic rotation, provide an explicit pause/resume control, pause on pointer hover, and stop when keyboard focus enters; do not restart after focus entry without user intent. Preserve focus on controls. Reduced-motion behavior and a non-drag alternative are required design considerations. These interaction constraints are supported by the [WAI carousel pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/carousel/); a still screenshot does not establish them.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CAR-01 | Anchored introduction and moving track: heading/controls hold one region while category cards occupy another. | Service exploration with a stable explanatory context. | Users must compare all offerings simultaneously. | Show continuation without clipping required text; move the introduction above a usable track/list on mobile. |
| CAR-02 | Feature slide plus thumbnail index: one detailed item is paired with a visible overview of available items. | Portfolios, products, or locations with rich individual content. | Thumbnails are indistinguishable or conceal meaningful labels. | Provide named selectors and selected state; keep a text alternative when thumbnail space disappears. |
| CAR-03 | Caption-first gallery: stable title/caption controls orient users while images change. | A set of images needs consistent contextual explanation. | Captions refer to the wrong image during transitions. | Update caption and media together; retain controls and avoid resizing the page for every image. |
| CAR-04 | Date or chapter selector: meaningful periods or topics select the corresponding content. | Histories, release stories, or ordered programs. | Dates are decorative and chronological order has no value. | Make the selected period readable and keyboard-operable; provide a linear version for long labels. |
| CAR-05 | Testimony with evidence: an attributed account is paired with the relevant project or artifact. | Supplied customer evidence needs more space than a quote grid. | Quotes, people, or outcomes would be invented. | Keep attribution visible and text selectable; allow enough reading time and manual progression. |
| CAR-06 | Item detail with adjacent preview: a partial next item signals sequence without requiring dot interpretation. | Products or projects with concise, comparable summaries. | Essential details are hidden in the cut-off portion. | Keep current content complete; ensure focus scrolls a reachable item into view and controls work without dragging. |
| CAR-07 | Labeled category tabs and content panel: users choose by meaning rather than repeated next clicks. | A small set of distinct service/product categories. | Independent content needs simultaneous comparison. | Use the existing tabs pattern if behaving as tabs; preserve selection and stable panel dimensions where practical. |
| CAR-08 | Filmstrip with inspection area: a compact strip supports selecting detailed media for closer viewing. | Product details, facilities, or visual references. | There is no meaningful detail beyond the thumbnail. | Maintain image descriptions and full-view access; use a native-feeling small-screen gallery with visible controls. |
| CAR-09 | Sequential case study: context, intervention, and outcome share one explicit step sequence. | A short, intentionally guided explanation. | Visitors need to scan all facts quickly for a decision. | Label step position and offer access to the complete story; never require timed auto-advance to understand the case. |
| CAR-10 | Horizontal archive with visible index: dates/titles provide direct access to many browsable entries. | Releases, exhibitions, or collections with strong item identities. | The archive grows too large for a track to search effectively. | Offer a full archive/list destination and keyboard-usable navigation; do not make swiping the only discovery method. |

## Modular grids (GRID)

Unequal spans should represent unequal roles or content needs. Choose an open list or normal grid when everything has the same responsibility. Do not create more modules merely to fill a bento silhouette.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GRID-01 | Lead story plus supporting pair: one dominant module anchors two subordinate contributions. | A featured project has clear editorial priority. | All entries must receive equal comparison weight. | Establish priority in DOM order; stack the lead before related material without stretching tiny supporting text. |
| GRID-02 | Visual anchor plus fact column: one authentic image accompanies a compact sequence of practical facts. | Spaces, products, or venues with relevant visual context. | Facts are fabricated or media is generic decoration. | Keep facts as real text; avoid trapping them inside an image crop and separate columns on mobile. |
| GRID-03 | Workflow mosaic: connected modules show successive stages with different evidence at each stage. | A process has genuinely different activities/artifacts. | The grid obscures the sequence it claims to explain. | Number or label stages and preserve reading order; present as a straight sequence on small screens. |
| GRID-04 | Directory modules: categories have clear names, descriptions, and counts where verified. | Collections have meaningful groups and browse destinations. | The grid becomes a nested set of anonymous icons. | Keep entire-card link semantics deliberate; allow category names to wrap and expose all destinations without hover. |
| GRID-05 | Calendar anchor with upcoming items: a time view relates to a short event list. | Events, availability, or programs are organized by date. | The calendar is decorative and does not correspond to actual data. | Synchronize selected dates and list scope; provide the list as a complete small-screen alternative. |
| GRID-06 | Comparison modules: a shared criterion spans the row while alternatives occupy consistent positions. | A small number of options have aligned differentiators. | Variable module shapes prevent accurate comparison. | Align repeated values and units; switch to a structured comparison view rather than arbitrary mobile card ordering. |
| GRID-07 | Artifact wall: real work samples differ in span according to content and editorial importance. | A portfolio contains varied authentic artifacts. | Every image is forced into an identical crop. | Preserve aspect-ratio intent, descriptive captions, and a coherent gap rhythm; ensure source order remains sensible. |
| GRID-08 | Map plus local services: spatial context anchors nearby destination modules. | Regional services or multiple sites support location-based browsing. | Geography does not influence the next action. | Keep addresses and service links available outside the map; stack practical details first on mobile. |
| GRID-09 | Guided choice grid: a short introductory module explains how to choose among remaining options. | Users need a selection framework before browsing. | The explanatory tile simply repeats the section heading. | Use comparable option descriptions and plain actions; keep instruction and choices adjacent in reading order. |
| GRID-10 | Editorial rhythm across rows: selected content spans columns while quiet rows reset the page. | Mixed stories need varied emphasis without theme changes. | Every module is highlighted so no hierarchy survives. | Reuse grid lines, spacing, and component vocabulary; collapse by content priority rather than visual packing order. |
