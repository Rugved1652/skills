# Product Interfaces

Read [Dashboards](#dashboards-dash), [Tables](#tables-tbl), [Statistics and charts](#statistics-and-charts-stat), or [Forms](#forms-form). These 40 original suggestions prioritize operational questions and decisions. Use existing data capabilities, components, and theme; do not add fake functionality or fabricated performance data to make a view look complete.

## Dashboards (DASH)

Before choosing a composition, complete: **This user needs to notice ___, decide ___, and do ___ within ___ context.** Select a main working surface from that answer. A dashboard can be mostly a list, queue, schedule, or table. Do not add an overview page when the user would be better served by opening their actual work.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DASH-01 | Exception-first workspace: actionable outliers lead; ordinary activity remains secondary. | Operators must identify delayed, blocked, or otherwise defined exception cases. | The urgency definition is invented by the designer. | Use real status rules and text labels; distinguish no exceptions from unavailable data and retain a full-list destination. |
| DASH-02 | Work queue plus inspector: prioritized items remain visible beside the selected case's details and actions. | Support, operations, or clinical work centers on item-by-item decisions. | Comparison needs a broad matrix or details require a full workflow. | Preserve selection and queue position; adapt to list/detail navigation on mobile with a clear return path. |
| DASH-03 | Timeline-centered overview: events, deadlines, and ownership share a time axis. | Scheduling and time dependencies drive the day's work. | Event order is not important or dates are unreliable. | Supply a text/list alternative, meaningful time zones, and access to overdue items beyond the visible interval. |
| DASH-04 | Analytical overview and drill-down: a focused question governs one summary and its supporting breakdowns. | Users investigate trends and contributing segments. | Several unrelated charts merely share spare space. | Keep filters and comparison periods explicit; make drill-down targets accessible and distinguish missing data from zero. |
| DASH-05 | Entity workspace: identity and state frame related sections for one account, project, or case. | Work is organized around a selected object. | An object-focused view is mislabeled as a global summary. | Keep identity stable through local navigation; avoid making its header a large decorative hero. |
| DASH-06 | Role-specific start page: current responsibilities and relevant destinations replace a universal metric wall. | Different roles enter the product to do different work. | The UI is guessing access or changing authorization rules. | Derive content from actual permissions and available capabilities; keep essential navigation consistent between roles. |
| DASH-07 | Schedule and capacity view: time slots or resource lanes show actual commitments and availability. | Bookings, staffing, equipment, or production planning. | The underlying capacity rules are unknown. | Provide a keyboard/list workflow, explicit conflicts, and readable narrow-screen scheduling without drag-only interactions. |
| DASH-08 | Comparison workspace: aligned entities and common criteria support a specific choice. | Reviewing vendors, cohorts, locations, or scenarios. | Decorative tiles make numerical comparison harder. | Preserve shared units, periods, and definitions; retain selection context and a workable small-screen criterion view. |
| DASH-09 | Execution board: stages organize items by a real workflow state. | Movement through stages is the primary management task. | Users mostly need sorting by date or exact numerical comparison. | Show stage meaning and work limits when real; offer non-drag transitions and a compact list alternative. |
| DASH-10 | Operational log with health context: ordered events and current service state support investigation. | Monitoring or incident work requires tracing changes. | A marketing dashboard is being dressed up as a live console. | Control live updates, preserve paused investigation context, and keep timestamps, severity, and unavailable telemetry explicit. |

## Tables (TBL)

Use tables for aligned comparison. Inspect installed sorting, filtering, pagination, selection, and virtualization behavior before changing presentation. Do not claim server capabilities the API lacks. Preserve semantic relationships or the existing accessible grid behavior; a dense operational table is not automatically improved by converting every row into a card.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TBL-01 | Identity-led record table: a strong name/identifier column anchors concise comparable attributes. | Users locate and open a known record. | Decorative icons and repeated avatars crowd out differentiating fields. | Keep long names readable; prioritize meaningful columns and preserve a stable detail-link target. |
| TBL-02 | Exception-sorted table: real issue states determine the initial ordering and visible cues. | Users need to act on defined problems first. | Severity is guessed or color is the only indicator. | Explain the active sort and allow appropriate alternatives; separate unknown state from healthy state. |
| TBL-03 | Grouped rows with subtotals: category boundaries and actual aggregates support scanning within a large set. | Users reason about department, date, or account groups. | Grouping conceals important cross-group comparison. | Label aggregate scope; retain expand controls and distinguish page totals from dataset totals. |
| TBL-04 | Comparison matrix: entities form one axis and stable criteria form the other. | A small set of alternatives needs explicit comparison. | The matrix has too many entities for usable scanning. | Keep row/column headers understandable; permit horizontal inspection without shrinking text below the existing scale. |
| TBL-05 | Selection and batch toolbar: selected scope determines the available collective actions. | Repeated operations can safely share one action. | Mixed permissions or statuses make the action ambiguous. | State count and eligibility, provide clear deselection, and disclose whether selection includes off-page results. |
| TBL-06 | Anchored identity column: the record identifier stays visible while users inspect a wide field set horizontally. | Wide operational records need exact cross-column inspection. | Pinned columns consume nearly all available content width. | Preserve header associations, visible scroll affordance, and focus outlines; offer a focused detail view when narrow-screen comparison becomes impractical. |
| TBL-07 | Review-and-edit table: a small set of supported fields can be edited in place with clear save state. | Users correct comparable values across many records. | Validation or dependencies require a richer form. | Distinguish viewing from editing, expose pending/error state, and support keyboard completion without accidental saves. |
| TBL-08 | Time-bucket table: related periods align across columns for exact change comparison. | Financial, staffing, or operational period review. | A trend shape matters more than precise values. | Keep units, missing values, totals, and period definitions consistent; use controlled horizontal scrolling as needed. |
| TBL-09 | Audit table: timestamp, actor, event, and target form an accountable change record. | Users investigate what changed and by whom. | Decorative activity cards obscure sequence or exact identifiers. | Keep time-zone context and readable event text; long identifiers need a usable wrap/copy approach. |
| TBL-10 | Change-review table: field names align original and proposed values with known conflicts or validation results. | Reviewing a supported import, reconciliation, or bulk edit before commitment. | Values are guessed or change provenance is unavailable. | Label original/proposed columns explicitly; preserve full values and issue context in a stacked narrow-screen review. |

## Statistics and charts (STAT)

Start with the question and data, then choose the encoding. Keep real units, periods, denominators, and missing-data distinctions. Semantic status colors must retain their product meaning. Do not invent a data palette, threshold, trend, or KPI; use the existing visualization system. If no suitable system exists, follow the design-system extension rules rather than scatter arbitrary chart colors.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STAT-01 | Contextual metric: a value is paired with its unit, scope, period, and meaningful comparison. | One genuinely important measure informs a decision. | A large number has no interpretable context. | Keep label/value legible together; distinguish unavailable data and avoid decorative count-up animations. |
| STAT-02 | Trend with annotation: a time series highlights a verified event or change relevant to interpretation. | Users need to understand movement over time. | An annotation claims causation not supported by evidence. | Provide accessible values or a table, readable axes, and non-hover access to the important annotation. |
| STAT-03 | Ranked comparison: aligned bars or rows compare entities on one meaningful measure. | Relative magnitude and order drive attention. | Different units or periods make ranking misleading. | Preserve shared scale and visible labels; allow long entity names and explicit treatment of ties/missing values. |
| STAT-04 | Target and actual: actual progress is shown against a genuine defined target. | Capacity, goals, or limits have real product meaning. | A decorative gauge invents a success threshold. | Name the target and period; distinguish over-target from desirable change according to domain rules, not automatic green/red. |
| STAT-05 | Distribution view: buckets or points expose spread instead of only a mean. | Variation, outliers, or concentration matters. | There is too little data to support the implied pattern. | Explain units and bucket meaning; keep a readable textual summary and access to underlying detail. |
| STAT-06 | Composition breakdown: labeled parts show how a known total is divided. | A few categories make up a meaningful whole. | Categories overlap or a long legend overwhelms comparison. | Keep totals and category values available; do not rely on slice color or tiny unlabeled segments. |
| STAT-07 | Small multiples: consistent mini-views share a scale for comparison across entities. | Repeated trends need pattern comparison. | Each chart secretly uses a different scale. | State shared scale and scope; reduce visible series on small screens without removing the full comparison path. |
| STAT-08 | Period comparison: comparable intervals or cohorts are aligned for direct evaluation. | Users ask how this period differs from a valid reference. | Incomplete periods or changed definitions are hidden. | Label intervals and completeness; use shape/line distinctions as well as permitted colors and include exact values. |
| STAT-09 | Status composition with detail access: an aggregate count leads to the actual affected records. | Monitoring needs quick orientation followed by action. | A summary badge replaces the only usable work queue. | Define category meaning, show unknown states, and ensure each relevant count has an accurately scoped destination. |
| STAT-10 | Relationship plot: two variables reveal associations and selectable groups. | Investigating relationships is an actual analytical task. | A correlation plot is used as proof of cause or as filler. | Label axes and units; provide accessible selections and a non-visual data path rather than relying on hover. |

## Forms (FORM)

Use the existing form, validation, and component conventions. Form layout follows the data relationship and task sequence; taste-design must not invent business validation, consent, or authorization rules.

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FORM-01 | Single-column task form: a direct sequence groups related fields and ends with one clear action. | A focused, moderate-length input task. | An arbitrary two-column arrangement splits related reading order. | Use persistent labels, useful helper text, and inline errors; retain values after failure and support long translations. |
| FORM-02 | Sectioned form with summary navigation: meaningful groups organize a long editable record. | Complex settings or applications benefit from direct section access. | A small form is being padded into an enterprise layout. | Surface hidden-section errors and unsaved changes; collapse the index on mobile without losing section orientation. |
| FORM-03 | Progressive steps: a sequence separates dependent decisions with visible progress and return paths. | Later choices depend on earlier answers. | Independent short fields are split to create artificial engagement. | Preserve inputs between steps, explain optionality, and show an accurate review/submit boundary. |
| FORM-04 | Choice-first branching: an initial meaningful choice reveals only relevant fields. | Different supported tasks need different input sets. | Branching hides consequences or infers unsupported business behavior. | Make branch labels clear; preserve or deliberately clear prior data according to actual form rules. |
| FORM-05 | Editable summary: current values lead, with a focused edit path for each section. | Returning users inspect more often than they change information. | Users must edit most fields on every visit. | Keep edit/save/cancel states explicit and restore the original summary on cancellation. |
| FORM-06 | Inline task composer: a short creation form sits where the resulting object will appear. | Adding a note, task, or simple record is part of ongoing work. | Sensitive or complex input needs a dedicated surface. | Preserve content on failure, identify the created item, and keep the next focus location predictable. |
| FORM-07 | Review-before-commit: entered information is presented in readable groups before final submission. | A consequential transaction benefits from confirming the full choice. | A trivial change receives an unnecessary extra step. | Provide section-level correction paths and accurate commitment language; avoid implying submission before the real action. |
| FORM-08 | Repeating item editor: structured rows or groups add comparable line items with totals when real. | Orders, allocations, or another supported repeated input model. | Unrelated complex objects are crammed into compact rows. | Give each item stable identity, accessible add/remove actions, and usable validation and mobile grouping. |
| FORM-09 | Import-and-resolve form: file/data selection leads into a mapping or issue-review stage. | The product actually supports structured bulk input. | An attractive upload zone promises nonexistent processing. | State accepted formats/limits from implementation; distinguish uploading, validation, partial issues, and completed import. |
| FORM-10 | Side-by-side preview: editing and a corresponding output share a work surface. | Content, formatting, or configuration has an immediate meaningful result. | Preview latency or invented output would mislead the user. | Make preview freshness clear; switch to edit/preview modes on small screens while preserving entered values. |
