# Navigation and Wayfinding

Read only the relevant category: [Navbar](#navbar-nav), [Sidebar](#sidebar-sid), [Search and filtering](#search-and-filtering-srch), or [Footer](#footer-ftr). Each category contains ten independent suggestions. Use [research](research.md) for outside examples and provenance.

All patterns inherit the current design system. Their names describe organization or behavior, not a new visual theme. Keep labels, active states, focus indicators, and touch targets consistent with existing components.

## Navbar (NAV)

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NAV-01 | Task bar: brand, short destination list, and one clearly separated primary action. | A small public site with one dominant visitor goal. | Several audiences need equally prominent starting points. | Collapse secondary links first; keep the primary action discoverable without filling the mobile viewport. |
| NAV-02 | Utility plus primary rows: separate account/location/help from the main information architecture. | Multilocation services or large institutions. | The second row would contain only decorative labels. | Merge utility links into a labeled mobile group; avoid two sticky rows consuming reading space. |
| NAV-03 | Category mega menu: group destinations by meaningful headings, with optional contextual preview. | A broad catalog whose categories users understand. | A few links fit in a normal disclosure. | Open by click/keyboard as well as pointer; provide a deliberate close path and a linear narrow-screen hierarchy. |
| NAV-04 | Audience entry navigation: first distinguish who the visitor is, then reveal relevant destinations. | Different customer groups have genuinely different journeys. | Visitors cannot confidently identify their category. | Keep a visible way to switch audience; avoid presenting ambiguous personas as a mandatory gate. |
| NAV-05 | Search-led header: give the query field priority and keep category links subordinate. | Documentation, inventory, or a large searchable collection. | The site has little searchable content or weak search capability. | Keep a visible search affordance on mobile; show scope, results, loading, and no-result feedback. |
| NAV-06 | Section navigator: a local contents row follows the page's main sections. | Long service, product, or explanatory pages. | It duplicates a short page's obvious reading sequence. | Account for sticky-header offsets; preserve deep links and visible focus without scroll hijacking. |
| NAV-07 | Workspace header: organization/project selector, context, and relevant global actions. | Users switch between accounts or projects during work. | A static marketing brand is being made to resemble software. | Show the current workspace plainly; preserve selection and expose switching through an accessible menu. |
| NAV-08 | Media-integrated navigation: position a compact header within a media composition. | A strong image-led opening with a restrained link set. | Contrast or a changing background makes labels unreliable. | Use system-supported contrast surfaces; provide a stable mobile header and unclipped focus outlines. |
| NAV-09 | Minimal header with full-screen directory: keep the shell quiet and reveal organized destinations on demand. | Editorial or portfolio browsing with modest navigation frequency. | A work app depends on rapid repeated destination changes. | Use explicit menu labeling, focus management, Escape/close behavior, and an immediate usable state without animation. |
| NAV-10 | Breadcrumb plus actions: locate a detail page within its hierarchy and align context actions separately. | Nested records, documentation, or catalog details. | History is being confused with a real hierarchy. | Preserve the current item and accessible overflow for ancestors; do not shrink every label to fit. |

## Sidebar (SID)

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SID-01 | Grouped destination list: labeled navigation groups with clear active item and restrained dividers. | Workspaces with several stable functional areas. | The groups exist only to fill empty sidebar height. | Persist explicit collapse choices when appropriate; retain readable labels and a usable mobile drawer. |
| SID-02 | Rail plus contextual pane: a compact app-area rail selects a second panel of local destinations. | Complex tools with distinct work modes and deep local navigation. | A single flat list would be faster to scan. | Show both area and local selection; provide names beyond icons and simplify to one mobile hierarchy. |
| SID-03 | Collapsible labeled sidebar: switch between full labels and a compact rail. | Users benefit from reclaiming horizontal workspace. | Narrow content remains cramped even after collapsing. | Animate orientation without losing focus; keep accessible names and persistent user control over expansion. |
| SID-04 | Project tree: expandable folders express actual nested ownership. | File, document, repository, or knowledge hierarchies. | Items are peers or have multiple incompatible parents. | Use established tree keyboard behavior where applicable; preserve expanded paths and selected-node visibility. |
| SID-05 | Queue sidebar: destination groups carry meaningful pending counts and urgency summaries. | Support, moderation, operations, or clinical triage. | Counts have no actionable meaning or are unreliable. | Distinguish unavailable counts from zero; use text alongside status color and avoid noisy live announcements. |
| SID-06 | Task-step sidebar: stages display progress and permit valid returns. | Long applications or structured setup flows. | Users actually need free-form navigation among independent tools. | Indicate current, completed, blocked, and optional stages; preserve entered data when revisiting a stage. |
| SID-07 | Inspector sidebar: a selected item opens properties alongside the main workspace. | Editors, design tools, or detail-heavy record review. | The panel replaces the product's only primary navigation. | Reserve enough width for labels; on small screens use a deliberate detail view with a clear return path. |
| SID-08 | Filter sidebar: facets and result scope live next to the collection. | Large catalogs or investigative data views. | Only one simple filter is needed. | Keep filter meaning separate from navigation; expose active filters and apply/reset controls in the mobile sheet. |
| SID-09 | Personal workspace sidebar: stable product destinations precede pinned/recent items. | Tools with repeatedly revisited user-selected content. | Personalization would obscure required shared destinations. | Label the two responsibilities; handle long names and allow keyboard-accessible pin/reorder alternatives. |
| SID-10 | Operational context panel: identity and essential context stay above the local navigation. | Record-centered work such as reviewing a case or account. | A large decorative profile block displaces daily tasks. | Keep context factual and compact; collapse secondary details before hiding the record identity. |

## Search and filtering (SRCH)

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SRCH-01 | Collection search: query, result count, and relevant filters share a toolbar. | A bounded list or table with clear search scope. | The same input is expected to search the entire product. | Label the scope; preserve the query while results update and provide distinct loading/empty/error feedback. |
| SRCH-02 | Global command search: a dialog finds destinations and actions as well as content. | Complex tools with frequent expert navigation. | It becomes the only way to discover ordinary functions. | Keep visible navigation alternatives, explicit result types, keyboard movement, and a reliable dismiss/return path. |
| SRCH-03 | Faceted exploration: combine independent attribute controls with visible applied filters. | Products, documents, or datasets with several useful dimensions. | The facets reflect database columns rather than user decisions. | Show selected values and reset scope; use a mobile filter panel without discarding selections on close. |
| SRCH-04 | Progressive advanced search: a simple query opens structured conditions when needed. | Most users search simply but experts need precision. | All users must fill an elaborate query builder first. | Make advanced mode optional; show the resulting scope and allow a clear return to basic search. |
| SRCH-05 | Saved views: named query/filter/sort combinations become reusable destinations. | Repeated operational queues or analytical perspectives. | The product cannot persist or distinguish these configurations. | Show unsaved changes and reset behavior; separate personal and shared view ownership. |
| SRCH-06 | Date-first analysis: time range and comparison period govern related modules together. | Reporting where time context drives most questions. | Only one unrelated widget needs a different period. | Make scope visible; use explicit dates/time zones and preserve a keyboard-usable range selection. |
| SRCH-07 | Typed result groups: present people, documents, and actions under separate headings. | A query legitimately spans different object classes. | Grouping hides the strongest match behind many categories. | Keep ranking understandable; provide type-specific follow-up and a concise small-screen ordering. |
| SRCH-08 | Search with preview: selecting a result reveals enough context before navigation. | Similar-looking records or documents that are costly to open blindly. | The preview contains no useful differentiating information. | Keep selection distinct from activation; offer a full detail destination and a stacked mobile alternative. |
| SRCH-09 | Map/list search: maintain a synchronized geographic view and result list. | Locations, venues, deliveries, or spatial inventory. | Geography does not affect the decision. | Make location results usable without the map; offer an explicit mobile toggle and avoid hover-only correspondence. |
| SRCH-10 | Recovery suggestions: no results expose removable constraints and valid next searches. | Narrow combinations frequently exclude all matching content. | Suggestions would invent available products or search capabilities. | Preserve the original query; distinguish no matches from loading failure and name exactly what each reset changes. |

## Footer (FTR)

| ID | Pattern and mechanism | Good fit | Avoid when | Adaptation and behavior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FTR-01 | Compact utility close: legal, contact, preferences, and a small identity anchor. | Short product pages or utility-focused sites. | Users depend on the footer as a larger resource directory. | Keep readable text and wrapping groups; avoid a giant brand panel that outweighs the page. |
| FTR-02 | Categorized directory: destination columns echo meaningful site areas. | Content-rich sites with several visitor journeys. | Most columns would contain one redundant link. | Preserve group headings; if mobile groups collapse, use disclosure controls with predictable keyboard access. |
| FTR-03 | Contact-led ending: address, availability, and a relevant inquiry action take priority. | Services whose next step is a conversation or visit. | Contact details are fabricated or a different task is primary. | Use selectable text and working links; retain essential contact information without decorative scrolling. |
| FTR-04 | Final decision band: a concise commitment prompt precedes quieter utility links. | A page has answered objections and supports a clear next step. | The footer introduces a different competing conversion. | Reuse the established action label; keep supporting conditions legible and avoid duplicating a large hero. |
| FTR-05 | Editorial continuation: curated related reading closes the page before site utilities. | Articles, research, or documentation with a natural next topic. | It traps users in unrelated promotional recommendations. | Show why content is related; stack entries by priority and retain clear article/link labels. |
| FTR-06 | Location directory: regions, venues, or service areas organize practical next steps. | Multilocation businesses with meaningful local differences. | Region links only repeat identical generic content. | Use concise location names; make address and local contact information accessible on small screens. |
| FTR-07 | Newsletter with expectations: subscription describes subject and cadence beside the form. | A publication or community has a real recurring offering. | Subscription is forced or claims cannot be substantiated. | Keep the label, consent context, validation, success, and unsubscribe expectations clear; do not auto-open a modal. |
| FTR-08 | Brand signature close: an identity treatment finishes an otherwise restrained information block. | A brand-led site with an existing expressive visual language. | Novelty requires changing fonts or burying practical links. | Scale the signature independently of utility text; keep essential controls outside clipping or decorative animation. |
| FTR-09 | Task-specific support footer: help, documentation, and status links conclude a work surface. | Settings, onboarding, or infrequently used operational pages. | An application already has equivalent persistent support access. | Keep it compact; never add a marketing sitemap beneath every dense application table. |
| FTR-10 | Local page ending: last-updated context, ownership, and a return-to-section action close a long reference. | Policies, technical documents, and service instructions. | Metadata is unverified or mistaken for legal certification. | Keep dates and ownership factual; scrolling controls must respect focus, document structure, and motion preferences. |
